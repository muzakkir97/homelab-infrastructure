### 2026-07-24 - Kinmoon Storage Pool 1 RAID rebuild: defer until libata.force=3.0Gbps fix applied
**Decision:** Do not attempt further Storage Pool 1 RAID 1 rebuilds until the underlying SATA link-speed hardware issue is resolved via kernel boot parameter. Two consecutive rebuild attempts (2026-07-24 01:32:29 and auto-resumed ~07:06) both failed deterministically ~18-19 seconds after Kinmoon boot with identical "failed command: WRITE FPDMA QUEUED" error on Hard Drive 2. This is not a drive health issue (Hard Drive 2 SMART status confirmed healthy, Reallocated Sector Count 99 vs. threshold 10); instead, it is a known UGREEN DXP2800 SATA compatibility issue where some drives have narrow signal timing tolerance at full 6.0Gbps link speed. Confirmed via UGREEN community forum and independent documentation (rack2reality.com, May 2026) that UGREEN support officially recommends forcing SATA to 3.0Gbps via kernel boot parameter `libata.force=3.0Gbps` appended to `linux /boot/vmlinuz` and `linux /boot/@VMLINUXZ@` lines in `/boot/EFI/debian/grub.cfg` and `/boot/EFI/debian/grub.am`. Deferred fix application until after emergency backup copy of 1.3TB vzdump archive to Kuromoon's hdd-backup-2 is verified complete (prioritize data safety over rebuild completion). Fix not yet applied as of session end.
**Why:** Rebuilding against a drive that fails 18-19 seconds post-boot will fail again, regardless of retry count. The RAID 1 rebuild is blocked by this timing issue, not by actual drive failure. Applying the fix requires a reboot and carries minor risk of boot-config syntax error; deferring until backup data is safe off Kinmoon eliminates the risk of losing the sole copy of Proxmox VM/container backup history during the fix attempt. This is a documented, confirmed hardware issue with an official workaround, not a blind troubleshooting guess.
**Alternatives considered:** Attempt a third rebuild without fixing the root cause (rejected — same deterministic failure would occur, wasting time), apply the fix immediately before backup completes (rejected — prioritize data safety), replace Hard Drive 2 as well (rejected — SMART data confirms it is healthy; the issue is SATA signal timing, not drive wear).

### 2026-07-24 - Kinmoon Hard Drive 1 replacement: swapped in 3TB Seagate IronWolf; confirmed RAID 1 tolerates mixed models
**Decision:** Replaced failed Hard Drive 1 with spare 3TB Seagate IronWolf (NAS-rated CMR, not a matched-model replacement for the original drive). Confirmed installed in Bay 1 by UGOS as "Normal," 2.7TB capacity, healthy SMART status on install. Also confirmed via manufacturer documentation that RAID 1 rebuild does not require both drives to be identical model/capacity, only sufficient total capacity and NAS-rated reliability. Skipped pre-install SMART check on the spare IronWolf, accepting minor unknown-wear risk, in order to stop the recurring degraded-pool notification sooner. This tradeoff was explicitly acknowledged at the time.
**Why:** Hard Drive 1 was showing progressive media failure (Reallocated Sector Count trending 573 → 609 → 1,963, SMART Index 5 at 133 vs. failure threshold 140). Replacement was necessary to restore RAID 1 redundancy. Having a NAS-rated spare on hand eliminated the need to order a new drive and enabled faster recovery. RAID 1's mirroring logic does not depend on identical drive hardware, only on capacity parity and reliability — documented in both Seagate and the original drive manufacturer's specifications.
**Alternatives considered:** Wait for matched-model replacement to arrive (rejected — extends degraded-pool risk window), bypass the spare and order OEM replacement (rejected — spare on hand enables faster action), perform SMART check on spare first (rejected — minor unknown-wear risk was acceptable tradeoff for eliminating degraded-pool notifications sooner).

### 2026-07-24 - Kinmoon emergency backup: copy 1.3TB vzdump archive to Kuromoon hdd-backup-2 pending RAID resolution
**Decision:** Initiated emergency copy of Kinmoon's entire `/mnt/pve/kinmoon-smb/proxmox-backups/` CIFS share (1.3TB of Proxmox VM/container backup history) to Kuromoon's `/mnt/hdd-backup-2/kinmoon-emergency-backup/` via `nohup rsync -av` (PID 3211563, log at `/root/kinmoon-copy.log` on Kuromoon, started 2026-07-24 21:05). Destination drive hdd-backup-2 confirmed mounted and available. Rationale: Kinmoon's Storage Pool 1 is currently degraded after two failed rebuild attempts; the 1.3TB backup archive is the sole copy of all Proxmox backup data (not mirrored to Kuromoon's primary storage as are other datasets). Until Kinmoon's RAID 1 rebuild is confirmed stable, the backup archive represents a single point of failure risk. Copy must be verified complete (source and destination `du -sh` match, log check for errors) before applying the libata.force=3.0Gbps fix, which requires a reboot and carries minor boot-config risk.
**Why:** Data safety takes priority. Proxmox VM and container backups are not replicated elsewhere; they are the only downstream recovery path if any VM/container is lost or corrupted. While Kinmoon's hardware is being debugged, isolating a copy of this archive to a second physical location eliminates the single-point-of-failure window. Once Kinmoon stabilizes, the decision to keep or delete the emergency copy (hdd-backup-2 has limited space) can be made with confidence.
**Alternatives considered:** Apply the SATA fix immediately and retry the rebuild (rejected — minor boot-config risk while the sole backup copy is at risk), continue relying on Kinmoon's single degraded pool without backup (rejected — unacceptable redundancy loss for critical backup data), back up only critical VMs and skip others (rejected — without knowing which containers are most important, safer to copy the entire archive).

### 2026-07-24 - Kinmoon backup-daily job: disabled during RAID rebuild to avoid write load contention
**Decision:** Disabled the Proxmox `backup-daily` scheduled vzdump job (`enabled 0` in `/etc/pve/jobs.cfg`, applied ~2026-07-24 01:30) to prevent the nightly 02:00 run from competing with Storage Pool 1 RAID 1 rebuild operations for I/O bandwidth on Hard Drive 2. Rebuilding RAID mirrors is I/O-intensive and timing-sensitive; adding simultaneous backup writes would further stress an already-failing SATA link. Job remains disabled as of session end; must be re-enabled once Kinmoon Storage Pool 1 is confirmed healthy. Also, Game Server Backup Coverage Gap decision (2026-07-23) requires appending CT 306 and CT 307 to the VMID list in the same re-enablement pass.
**Why:** RAID 1 rebuild integrity depends on minimizing competing I/O load. Given that Hard Drive 2 is already subject to a known SATA timing issue that causes deterministic failures at 18-19 seconds post-boot, adding 02:00 backup writes during the rebuild window increases the risk of drive timeouts or rebuild failure. Once the SATA fix is applied and rebuild completes successfully, re-enabling backups restores normal VM/container protection.
**Alternatives considered:** Let backup-daily run and rely on RAID rebuild prioritization (rejected — no guarantee scheduler would prioritize rebuild over scheduled jobs), manually run backups after rebuild completes (rejected — less reliable than scheduled automation), use a separate backup window later in the day (rejected — administrative overhead, simpler to just disable and re-enable as one toggle).

### 2026-07-23 - Kinmoon Hard Drive 1 failure: do not attempt RAID rebuild until physical replacement
**Decision:** Do not click "Repair" on Kinmoon's Storage Pool 1 (RAID 1) until Hard Drive 1 is physically replaced with a healthy drive. Root cause of Hard Drive 1 failure: genuine progressive media failure, evidenced by Reallocated Sector Count trending March 573 → April 609 → May 1,963, with current SMART Index 5 at 133 (below failure threshold 140). UGOS RAID 1 pool status shows Degraded; Hard Drive 1 SMART status Critical. Current operational state: RAID 1 running on single healthy drive (Hard Drive 2) with zero redundancy. Risk: if Hard Drive 2 fails before replacement, entire proxmox-backups offsite backup archive (2.4TB, only downstream copy) would be lost. Correct action sequence: (1) source and physically replace Hard Drive 1, (2) then trigger UGOS Storage app Repair function to rebuild the mirror. Doing Repair now would either be a no-op against a failing drive or add further read/write stress to Hard Drive 1, risking acceleration of the failure.
**Why:** RAID rebuild (triggered by Repair) requires a healthy second member to rebuild onto. Attempting it against Hard Drive 1 in its current degraded state violates the principle of "diagnose before acting" — SMART data already gives a clear hardware diagnosis without requiring a destructive test. This matches the successful pattern used on Kuromoon's hdd-backup-1 SATA issue (2026-07-14), where investigation led to a specific root cause that could be acted on precisely rather than blindly.
**Alternatives considered:** Click Repair immediately to see if the pool self-heals (rejected — SMART data already shows progressive media failure, not a transient fault that Repair can resolve), replace the drive before investigating (rejected — understanding the failure mode prevents repeat hardware selection errors), rebuild onto Hard Drive 2 by converting to RAID 0 (rejected — would delete all backup history; defeats the purpose).

### 2026-07-23 - Kinmoon-smb NAS recycle bin cleanup deferred pending Hard Drive 1 replacement
**Decision:** Defer the kinmoon-smb NAS-level recycle bin toggle/cleanup task (`#recycle` folder, 1.3TB) in favor of prioritizing the Hard Drive 1 replacement first. Current impact of recycle bin: 1.3TB of files already logically freed by Proxmox's `prune-backups` retention policy (keep-daily=7, keep-weekly=4) are double-counted in NAS storage metrics, making the proxmox-backups share report 94% full (2.5TB used / 2.7TB capacity) when actual useful backup data is only ~1.3TB. This is a space-pressure issue, not a data-safety issue. Hard Drive 1's failure is a live risk to backup redundancy. Proxmox retention policy is confirmed working correctly; the recycle bin is cosmetic overhead.
**Why:** Addressing backup redundancy (Hard Drive 1 replacement) prevents data loss; clearing the recycle bin only improves headroom. Both are now tracked as separate action items; Hard Drive 1 takes priority sequentially. The recycle bin cleanup is straightforward once prioritized (likely via UGOS Storage app's per-share settings, path not yet fully mapped), but is not blocking any critical operation.
**Alternatives considered:** Perform both cleanup and hardware investigation in the same session (rejected — user elected to close out session after Hard Drive 1 discovery and summarize findings), ignore the recycle bin entirely (rejected — 1.3TB unused space is worth recovering once Hard Drive 1 is resolved and NAS capacity confidence is restored).
